# F42 — Map and explorer: notes placed by their relationships, not folders

**Status:** draft design, 2026-11-02. Nothing here is built. A clickable mockup is in
[F42-mockup.html](F42-mockup.html) (open it in a browser; made-up notes, nothing saved). David's decisions are recorded
below; the two calls I made on his behalf are marked *proposed* and are his to change.
It builds on relationships (F31: `src/relationships.ts`, `src/relationshipIndex.ts`, the
collection's `Relationships.md`), types (F16) and the graph (`web/components/Graph*.tsx`).

## Why

A folder puts a note in one place, and the place has to be chosen when the note is made. Most
notes belong to more than one thing (the tiles note is part of the kitchen project *and* about a
supplier), and the folder hides every place but one. DO Notes already records what a note is part of,
depends on or is about. Placing notes by those relationships means a note shows wherever it
belongs, and moving it means saying something true about it rather than dragging a file.

## In a sentence

The left panel's folder tree becomes an **explorer** built from relationships, the **map** draws
the same structure as stable areas you can find your way round, and existing folders become **hub
notes** that their notes are `part-of`; collections stay as the outer boundary, because they
decide who can see what.

## Decided (David, 2026-11-02)

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| What builds the tree | **Any relationship**, not only `part-of` |
| The map | **A stable map of areas** (positions kept per collection) |
| Existing folders | **Converted to hub notes**, with a preview and Undo |
| Exports | **Folders rebuilt from `part-of`** |
| Boards (the canvas, F11) | **The map replaces them** (after trying the mockup) |
| Existing boards | **Converted to boxes**, with a preview and Undo |
| Text, link and picture cards | **Become notes** in the box |
| Obsidian's `.canvas` | **Still exported**, one file per top-level box, laid out as on the map; importing one converts it |

## The explorer

```
▾ 🏠 House
   ▾ 🍳 Kitchen project
      Parts
        • Kitchen plan
        • Tiles            also under Howdens
      Depends on it
        • Budget 2027
   ▸ 🌱 Garden
▸ 🏢 Suppliers
Lenses:  Types · Daily notes · Inbox · Loose notes · Files
```

- **Which way a relationship nests.** Every relationship has a direction, so "any relationship"
  needs a rule for which end sits under which. The note that *names* the other sits under it
  (Tiles says `part-of: [[Kitchen project]]` → Tiles is under Kitchen project; Budget says
  `depends-on: [[Kitchen project]]` → under it, in "Depends on it"). Container-direction kinds
  (`has-part`, `source-of`, `author-of`) nest the other way, so writing either half gives the
  same tree.
- *Proposed:* **symmetric kinds don't nest** (`relates-to`, `contradicts`): neither end is above
  the other, so they show as lines on the map and in the note's side panel, not in the tree.
- *Proposed:* **a "Nests" column in `Relationships.md`** (yes / reversed / no), defaulting as
  above, so a collection can say its own kinds nest or don't, and the choice travels with the
  files.
- **Grouped by kind.** Under a note, children are grouped by the relationship ("Parts", "Depends
  on it", "Referenced by"), with Parts first; a group with one kind shows no heading. This is what
  keeps "any relationship" readable.
- **Roots** are the notes nothing places under anything else: in a typical household collection,
  House, People, Suppliers.
- **A note under two things shows in both**, with *also under …* on the second. A cycle (A part
  of B part of A) stops where it repeats, marked ↺, rather than expanding forever.
- **Lenses** beside the tree, for what isn't a relationship: **Types** (every type and its
  notes), **Daily notes**, **Inbox**, **Loose notes** (no relationships yet: the to-file list)
  and **Files** (attachments).
- **Moving is saying.** Drag a note onto another to make it `part-of` it (replacing its old
  `part-of`); hold ⌥ to add a second parent instead; drop on a root's gap to remove it. Each is
  an ordinary property edit through `notes.persist`, so it's in history, syncs, and can be undone.
  ⌘K → **Place under…** does the same from the keyboard and on the iPhone.

## The map

```
┌──────────── House ────────────┐   ┌──── Suppliers ────┐
│  ┌── Kitchen project ──┐      │   │                   │
│  │  ● Plan   ● Tiles ──┼──────┼───┼──● Howdens        │
│  │  ● Budget           │      │   │                   │
│  └─────────────────────┘      │   └───────────────────┘
│  ┌── Garden ──┐               │
└───────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Areas are the tree.** A root is an area, its children sit inside it, and so on down. A note
  in two places is drawn in its **first `part-of` parent**, with a +1 badge and a line to the
  other. Other relationships and plain links are thin lines, labelled when the note is selected,
  and can be turned off (Links).
- **Arrange, on or off** (David, 2026-11-02; a switch in the map's controls, remembered per
  person and collection, on to start).
  - **On:** a note's place is *which box it's in and its order there*: each box lays its notes
    out in rows (three cards wide, boxes side by side; two wide and stacked on a phone), so
    boxes never overlap, and while something is dragged the others slide aside to show where it
    will land.
  - **Off:** notes and boxes stay exactly where they're dropped (x and y), a box grows to fit
    what it holds, nothing makes room, and overlaps are yours to tidy.
  - **Switching** keeps what you see: off keeps everything where it is; on orders each box's
    notes by where they were, reading left to right, top to bottom.
  - Both are kept per collection (`map_layout`: vault, note, order, x, y; view state, so not in
    the Markdown).
- **⇶ Tidy, as Floe does** (tried in the mockup, 2026-11-02): ELK's layered layout with
  Floe's options (`~/Developer/floe` `autoLayout.ts`), left to right or top to bottom. Boxes stay
  real containers, connected notes sit side by side and lines are routed in right angles round
  what's between; it places everything, so Arrange goes off, and the routed lines are kept until
  something moves. Good for seeing a chain of relationships; less good as the everyday map, since
  notes with no lines stack in one column and the shape changes whenever the links do.
- **Lines show the kind of relationship by pattern and end mark, not colour alone** (so they
  read in dark mode, printed, and for people who don't see colour well):

  | Relationship | Line | End |
  |---|---|---|
  | In a second place (`part-of`) | solid, accent | ◆ at the box it's also in |
  | Depends on | dashed, orange | ▶ at what it needs |
  | References | dotted, blue | › at what it points at |
  | Relates to | solid, grey | ● at both ends (it goes both ways) |

  A collection's own kinds (from `Relationships.md`) take the next free pattern and colour; a
  "Line" column there can choose. Hovering a line names it ("Tiles references Topps Tiles");
  a selected note's lines are labelled.
- **Line shape is a choice:** curved (to start), straight, or right angles with rounded corners,
  in the map's **Lines** menu, which also turns each kind on or off. Lines join notes at their
  edges (a box at its title bar), on the sides with the wider gap, and run behind notes.
- **Not in anything yet:** notes with no place sit in a dashed tray under the areas, the map's
  version of the Inbox.
- **Moving is dragging** (what the mockup settled):
  - grab a note or a box anywhere on it (on a phone, press and hold first, so a swipe still moves
    the map); with Arrange on, the others slide aside to show where it will land;
  - a note dropped in a box → it's now part of that box (its order is where you dropped it);
  - a box dropped → it moves where it is (its order, with Arrange on), or leaves its box if dropped
    outside it; it
    goes *inside* another box only when held over it (so boxes aren't nested by accident);
  - hold over a single note for half a second → it opens to take the drop (as Finder opens a
    folder), so a note can start holding others;
  - hold ⌥ → *also* put in, keeping the first place;
  - drop in the tray → taken out;
  - a label by the pointer says which will happen ("Put in Garden", "Also put in Howdens",
    "Change the order"), and every move has Undo.
- **Group:** ⇧-click several notes, then ⌘G (or the bar that appears) makes a new note holding
  them, as Heptabase makes a section.
- **Link:** drag from the ○ on a note's edge to another note and pick the relationship from a
  short menu (Part of, Depends on, References, Relates to).
- **Zoom by area.** Scroll or pinch to zoom; double-click an area to fill the screen with it;
  titles appear as there's room. Click a note to open it beside the map; the explorer and the map
  follow each other, and opening a note brings it into view with the smallest move.
- **Colours and icons from types**, as the graph already uses.
- The existing force graph stays for exploring links (it answers a different question).

## Retiring folders

1. **Convert, with a preview.** Settings → Collection → **Use relationships instead of
   folders…** shows what will happen: each folder becomes a hub note (`Kitchen/` → note
   *Kitchen*, keeping any `Kitchen/Kitchen.md` or `index.md` as the hub itself); every note in it
   gets `part-of: [[Kitchen]]`; nested folders become hubs `part-of` their parent hub.
2. **Names must be unique without folders.** Two notes called *Overview* become *Overview
   (Kitchen)* and *Overview (Garden)*, and links to them are fixed. The preview lists every rename.
3. **Paths go flat.** Notes keep a file name (exports, GitHub and history need one) but no
   folder; links written with a path (`[[Kitchen/Plan]]`) are rewritten to the title.
4. **Undo.** One change, recorded as a restore point, undone from the same place for 30 days.
5. **Kept as places, not folders:** `Types/`, `Relationships.md`, the daily notes folder, the
   Inbox and attachments. The app needs them, and they show as lenses.
6. **New notes** have no folder. ⌘K → New note asks nothing; New note *under this* (in the
   explorer or on the map) adds the `part-of` for you.

## Replacing boards (the canvas)

The map does what boards are for (cards you arrange, groups, arrows, one note in many places),
but from the notes' own relationships, so nothing on it is lost when you leave it. A board is
just a box: **open a box as its own map** (⋯ → Open as map, or double-click and keep going) and
that's the board.

**Converting a board** (Settings → Collection → **Turn boards into boxes…**, with the same kind
of preview and Undo as folders):

| On the board | On the map |
|---|---|
| The board | A box (hub note) with the board's name |
| A note card | That note, now part of the box (it keeps any other places it has) |
| A board card (a board on a board) | A box inside the box |
| A group | A box inside the box, named after the group's label |
| A text card | A short note with its text, in the box (titled from its first line) |
| A link card | A Weblink note, in the box |
| A picture | A note showing the picture, in the box |
| An arrow with a label | A relationship of that kind (offered as a new kind if the collection hasn't one) |
| An arrow without a label | *Relates to* |
| Where cards were | The box's order (reading order), and their places for Arrange off |
| Colours | Dropped; types give the colour |

The `.canvas` file is kept in the restore point, so Undo puts the board back exactly.

**What boards did that the map must keep:**

| Boards | The map |
|---|---|
| Several people arranging at once | Live moves and selections, as boards had (the map's order and places sync like a note) |
| Offline | Kept on the device and merged when back, as boards were |
| Arrows on this board (a list, for screen readers) | **Relationships here**: every line in the box as a sentence, from the Lines menu and ⌘K |
| Connect to… without dragging | ⌘K → **Relate to…** on the selected note |
| Mind map from here | ⇶ Tidy from one note: the notes joined to it, laid out to the right (Floe's layout) |
| The small map in the corner | Kept, on a laptop |
| A note's "On boards" | Its **In** list in the side panel |

**Export:** each top-level box is also written as a `.canvas` file (positions as on the map,
lines as labelled arrows), so a collection still opens as boards in Obsidian; importing a
`.canvas` converts it as above.

## What else changes

| Area | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Export** (zip, Obsidian) | Folders rebuilt from each note's first `part-of` chain; notes with none at the top. Hubs become `Folder/Folder.md`. Importing such a zip converts back the same way. |
| **GitHub and Mac folder collections** | They mirror real files, so files stay where they are: the explorer still builds from relationships, and conversion adds `part-of` without moving anything. |
| **Published sites (F18)** | Navigation from the `part-of` tree instead of folders. |
| **Standalone file** | Same explorer, from the same data. |
| **Search, links, backlinks** | Unchanged: they never depended on folders. |
| **AI tools (MCP)** | `list_notes` gains the parents; a `place_note` write tool (with `preview`) sets `part-of`. |
| **iPhone** | The explorer is the left drawer; the map is pinch-and-pan, view only in step 1. |

## Steps (each useful on its own)

1. **Explorer beside folders (M):** the relationship tree and lenses, with a switch in the left
   panel (Folders / Relationships), drag and ⌥-drag, Place under…. Nothing converted.
2. **Convert folders to hubs (M):** the preview, renames, link fixes, Undo; new notes without
   folders.
3. **The map (L):** areas, stable positions, zoom, explorer and map in step.
4. **Exports, import and publishing from `part-of` (S).**
5. **Retire the folder view (S):** once converted, the Folders switch goes; collections that
   aren't converted keep it until they are.
6. **The map takes over from boards (M):** open a box as its own map, live and offline moves,
   Relationships here, Relate to…, Tidy from one note, the small map.
7. **Turn boards into boxes (M):** the conversion, its preview and Undo; `.canvas` export per
   top-level box and import into boxes; then New board goes from ⌘K and the boards help page
   becomes *The map*.
